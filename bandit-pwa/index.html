Ten Thumbnails,
One Click.

by DEEP KOSHIYA

A streaming platform is launching a new show and has ten candidate thumbnail designs. Each thumbnail has its own hidden probability of getting a click when shown to a viewer — unknown to the platform, fixed by audience taste. Every time a viewer loads the page, the system must pick one thumbnail to display. This is the exact shape of the 10‑armed bandit problem: repeated choices under uncertainty, where the only way to learn a thumbnail's true click rate is to show it — and every show you spend on a bad one is a click you didn't earn from a better one.
